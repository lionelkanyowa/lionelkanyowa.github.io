# Project context: who this site is for

*Project-scoped adaptation of Lionel's personal `me.md`. This is the reference for
voice, audience, and boundaries on the website revamp. It is a trimmed, public-facing
subset — the full personal `me.md` stays private and is not a source for site copy.*

## The person behind the site

Lionel Kanyowa — a network/systems administrator at a nonprofit in Michigan, moving
into software engineering. Infrastructure-first background (identity, endpoints,
networking, a real home lab he runs because he enjoys it), B.S. Informatics, largely
self-taught in what he uses day to day.

He is now studying software engineering through Launch School's Core curriculum
(currently the Ruby track), mastery-based rather than speed-based. The site documents
that journey. He is deliberate, principle-first, and values understanding *why*
something works over collecting credentials.

## What the site is

A personal portfolio and public journal of the software-engineering journey. Not a
résumé dump. Its jobs, in order:

1. Show who he is and where he's headed (systems background → software engineering).
2. Document the learning in the open — a study log, longer writing, and videos.
3. Give employers, peers, and fellow learners a real sense of how he thinks.

Audience: future collaborators and employers, other Launch School / self-taught
learners, and his own future self as a record of the work.

## Voice and tone

- Direct and unhyped. No marketing gloss, no "passionate about synergy," no emoji as
  decoration. Plain, confident, specific.
- First person, present tense. Short paragraphs. American spelling. Sentence-case
  headings.
- Let the work speak. Prefer concrete detail ("rewrote my notes on iteration until I
  could explain it") over adjectives ("incredibly passionate learner").
- Calm and mature, not performative. He plans in years, not sprints — the copy can too.

## Public vs private boundary

Some of what makes him him is private. On the public site:

- **Fine to reference lightly and authentically:** he keeps Sabbath (Friday sunset to
  Saturday sunset) and it shapes his cadence; faith is part of who he is; he values
  rest and mastery over speed. Touch it honestly, never as generic "work-life balance,"
  never as the centerpiece.
- **Keep private / never publish:** devotional and religious study material, personal
  psychology and self-assessment, health, family, salary, employer specifics and
  internal-systems detail, the relocation timeline, and anything from the personal
  `me.md` not explicitly cleared here.
- When unsure whether something belongs on the public site, leave it out and ask.

## The language question (important for design)

The site is built with Jekyll (Ruby) and its frontend is HTML/CSS/JavaScript. Launch
School's current track is Ruby. **None of this should make the visual design "about" a
programming language.** No language mascots, gem motifs, or syntax-highlight-as-brand.
The design is about the person and the journey. Languages are tools mentioned as facts,
not identity.

## How Lionel likes to collaborate

*Applies to how the assistant works with him on this project.*

- Talk to him like a knowledgeable colleague who respects his time. No preamble, no
  "great question," no restating his request back to him.
- Give a recommendation with reasoning, not a menu of ten options. If there's a real
  fork, give two and say which you'd pick.
- Before big work, show the outline or approach first, then wait.
- Push back. If a plan has a hole, say so in the first sentence. Honest disagreement
  over validation.
- He self-corrects fast and builds scaffolding before the work — sometimes that's
  productivity, sometimes it's avoidance. If a meta-project is displacing real work,
  name it.
- Don't invent facts, numbers, or quotes for the site. Everything that ships under his
  name gets checked.
